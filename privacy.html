Privacy policy

Last updated August 29, 2026

The short version

Prosody doesn't have a server. There's no account, no cloud sync, no analytics or crash-tracking SDKs in the app. Your voice is processed on your phone. Your coding session's transcript never leaves the machines you already own. The only third-party network call Prosody's own software can make is one you turn on yourself, using your own API key.

  • Voice — processed on your iPhone, on-device. Never uploaded.
  • Coding session data — stays on your own gateway and agent machines.
  • Accounts — none exist. Pairing is a code between your phone and your gateway.
  • Analytics / tracking — none in the app or on this site.
  • Optional third party — Anthropic's API, only if you enable it with your own key.

What Prosody is

Prosody is two pieces of software: an iOS app, and a small "gateway" program you run yourself on a computer you own — a Mac mini, a laptop, a Linux workstation. The app on your phone talks directly to your gateway over your own network. There is no Prosody-operated server sitting in between. The people who build Prosody cannot see your coding sessions, your voice, or your approval decisions, because none of it passes through anything we run.

Voice

Speech recognition and speech synthesis both run on your iPhone, using Apple's on-device Speech framework — the app explicitly requests on-device recognition, not Apple's cloud fallback. Audio from your microphone is never sent to Prosody, and, because recognition happens on-device, it is not sent to Apple's servers either. There is currently no setting that changes this: no cloud speech-to-text or text-to-speech provider is active in the shipped app.

Your coding session

The gateway reads your coding agent's own session/transcript files directly from disk on the machine it runs on, and relays session state, turns, and approval requests to your phone over a TLS-encrypted connection between your phone and your gateway. That connection is trusted the first time you pair your phone with a gateway; there's no login and no account for anyone, including us, to manage.

Session data lives only on hardware you control: your gateway's own local state, and your coding agent's own transcript files (for example, Claude Code's local project logs), which already existed on your machine before Prosody touched them. Prosody does not copy this data anywhere else.

The one optional third-party call

Prosody can optionally rewrite a long agent turn into a shorter, more conversational sentence before speaking it aloud, using Anthropic's API. This feature:

  • is off by default;
  • only runs if you configure your own Anthropic API key on your own gateway;
  • sends only the text of the current turn being spoken — never audio, never your full transcript, never anything else from your session; and
  • falls back automatically to an on-gateway, non-networked text shaper if the key is missing or the call fails — a spoken summary is never skipped, and no error is ever read aloud in its place.

If you never set an API key, this call never happens.

What Prosody does not do

  • No accounts or sign-in of any kind.
  • No analytics, crash reporting, or usage-tracking SDKs in the app.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No server operated by Prosody's developer that sees your sessions, your voice, or your approval decisions.
  • No tracking scripts, cookies, or third-party embeds on this website.

Apple and TestFlight

Prosody is currently distributed through Apple's TestFlight program. Installing and running an app through TestFlight is subject to Apple's own privacy practices — for example, diagnostic and crash data the operating system may collect on Apple's behalf. That handling is Apple's, governed by Apple's own privacy policy; Prosody adds no tracking of its own on top of it.

Changes to this policy

Prosody has no account system and no mailing list, so we can't email you when this changes. The date at the top of this page is authoritative — if it changes, so did the policy. Any change that alters what leaves your device will be described here in plain terms, the same way it is today.
